You're finally cleaning out the garage, pulling up old carpet or redoing the kitchen. You know you need a dumpster. But if you care about your home and your driveway, a few questions are probably holding you back.

*Will a big truck crack my concrete? Will the HOA send me a letter? Do I need a permit?*

We're based right here in Fayette County, and we hear these questions all the time. Here are straight answers.

## 1. Will a Dumpster Ruin My Driveway?

It's the biggest worry we hear, and it's a fair one. A lot of waste companies use big, heavy commercial trucks. When one of those backs onto a home driveway, it can crack concrete or tear up the yard.

### How We Do It

We built our service around home driveways. Instead of a big commercial truck, we use a **Ford Super Duty hook-lift truck**.

- **Smaller truck:** it's lighter and easier to maneuver than a full-size roll-off truck. We look at the spot first, then back in and set the dumpster where you want it.
- **Free driveway boards:** ask and we'll put boards down under the dumpster at no charge.
- **Small footprint:** every one of our dumpsters is 12 feet long and 8 feet wide. In a lot of driveways, that still leaves room for a car or a golf cart beside it.

## 2. Do I Need a Permit in Peachtree City or Fayetteville?

**Usually not, if it's on your own property.** A dumpster in your driveway generally doesn't need a city or county permit. If your driveway is too steep or too short and the dumpster has to go in the street, Peachtree City has a right-of-way encroachment permit for that. Call the city before you schedule.

### Don't Forget the HOA

The city may not mind a dumpster in your driveway, but your HOA might. Some Fayette County HOAs are strict about how things look.

- **Best move:** send your HOA board a quick email with your project dates.
- **Ask about limits:** check how many days it can stay and where it has to sit.
- **It helps that it's pink:** nobody wants to look at a rusty, dented dumpster for a week. Ours are clean and bright, and the neighbors usually like seeing them.

## 3. Can't I Just Haul It to the Dump Myself?

If you have a pickup, a few free Saturdays and patience for the line at the transfer station, you could. But add up the gas, the dump fees, loading and unloading the truck over and over, and the scratches on your truck bed. It adds up fast.

With a dumpster, you load it once at your own pace, we haul it away, and you get your weekends back.
