Sooner or later, most growing contractors ask the same question: should we just buy our own dump trailer? It makes sense on paper. You save on dump runs, you control the schedule, and you own the equipment.

But the **dumpster vs. dump trailer** decision is about more than the purchase price. It's about cash, liability and keeping your crew on paid work. Once you add up what ownership really costs, **renting a dumpster** is often the better deal for contractors. Here's how to think it through.

## What Ownership Really Costs

### The purchase

A dump trailer is a big up-front purchase, and it loses value every year. That's money that could have gone into tools, payroll or marketing.

A rental has no purchase. You pay for it when a job needs it. (Ask your accountant how each option works for your business.)

### The yearly costs

These keep coming whether the trailer is working or parked:

- **Insurance:** higher commercial auto premiums to cover the trailer.
- **Registration and tags:** yearly fees, plus the time to keep up with them.
- **Maintenance:** tires, bearings, batteries and hydraulics all wear out. A breakdown means a repair bill and a delayed job.
- **Storage:** you need a safe place to keep it between jobs.

Add those up for a year and compare them to what you'd spend on rentals for the same jobs.

## Downtime

With one trailer, two situations cause trouble:

1. **Busy season.** Two demo jobs at once, one trailer. Your crew ends up driving debris around instead of doing billable work.
2. **Slow season.** The trailer sits while you keep paying insurance, registration and upkeep.

Renting lets you have a dumpster on each job when you're busy and none when you're slow, as long as one is available on your dates.

## License Rules: The Cost Nobody Mentions

This is the biggest hidden risk of owning a dump trailer.

A heavy-duty pickup pulling a mid-size dump trailer can cross into Commercial Driver's License (CDL) territory. Under the federal rule Georgia follows, a Class A CDL is generally required when the combined weight rating is 26,001 pounds or more and the trailer itself is rated over 10,000 pounds.

A common 14,000-pound dump trailer behind a heavy-duty pickup rated over 12,000 pounds hits that line. That means CDL licensing, higher insurance, DOT medical cards and roadside inspections. Check your own truck and trailer ratings with the [Georgia Department of Driver Services](https://dds.georgia.gov/) before you buy.

## One Size vs. the Right Size

Owning a trailer locks you into one size. If you have a 14-yard trailer, that's what every job gets.

Too big, and you're hauling half-empty loads. Too small, and your crew stops working to make extra dump runs.

Renting lets you match the dumpster to the job. We have three sizes, the [11](/11-yard-the-little-junker), [16](/16-yard-the-mighty-middler) and [21-yard](/21-yard-the-big-junker), and all three are 12 feet long and 8 feet wide. That makes them easy to place in home driveways and tight job sites.

## Your Time Is Worth More

You get paid to build and manage projects, not to wait in landfill lines or fix trailer lights.

With a rental:

1. **We deliver it** to the job site.
2. **We haul it.** When the job's done or the dumpster's full, one call or text gets it picked up.
3. **We handle the landfill**, including the weigh tickets.

## How It Looks to Your Customers

A clean dumpster dropped off on schedule makes a job site look organized. A broken-down trailer, or one getting shuffled between jobs, causes delays your customer notices. Renting also keeps your costs predictable as work picks up or slows down.

## Run the Numbers

Take a month of real jobs. Add up trailer payments, insurance, upkeep, dump runs and crew hours, then compare that to rental costs for the same jobs.
