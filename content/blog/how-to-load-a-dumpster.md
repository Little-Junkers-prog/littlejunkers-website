Loading a dumpster is a little like Tetris. Throw everything in at random and you'll run out of room fast. Load it with a plan and the same dumpster holds a lot more. Here are five tips that help.

### 1. Sort Before You Toss

**Why it matters:** a jumbled pile wastes space.

**How to do it:**

- **Make keep, toss and recycle piles** before you load anything. Grouping wood, metal, cardboard and general trash lets you pack like things tightly.
- **Break down bulky stuff** like boxes, old furniture and plastic pipe. A flattened box takes up a fraction of the room.

> *Tip:* Tie flattened cardboard with twine or tape so it doesn't spring back open.

### 2. Heavy Stuff Goes in First

**Why it matters:** a solid base keeps the load from shifting.

**How to do it:**

- **Put dense materials on the bottom:** tile, shingles, plaster, solid wood furniture. Concrete and dirt can't go in our dumpsters, so leave those out.
- **Spread the weight** across the whole floor of the dumpster, front to back and side to side.

> *Tip:* Ask us to set the dumpster close to your heaviest pile so you don't carry it as far.

### 3. Stack It Flat and Tight

**Why it matters:** floor space goes fast.

**How to do it:**

- **Run long pieces** like lumber, PVC pipe and trim along the sides.
- **Lay big sheets flat** (drywall, plywood, paneling) and stack them like a deck of cards. Don't stand them up loose, where they can tip over or fall against the door.
- **Stop at the top rail.** The load has to be level with the top for us to haul it safely.

### 4. Fill the Gaps

**Why it matters:** little empty pockets add up.

**How to do it:**

- **Toss in odd scraps** like wood cutoffs, tarps and insulation after the big stuff is in. They fill the holes between larger pieces.
- **Squash soft stuff** like rugs, old clothes and carpet pad so it takes as little room as possible.

> *Tip:* Use a shovel or a long board to push each layer down. Don't climb into the dumpster to stomp it.

### 5. Pick the Right Size for What You're Tossing

**Why it matters:** guess too small and you may need a second dumpster or extra days.

**How to do it:**

- **Bulky but light** (furniture, boxes, household junk)? If you're stuck between two sizes, go up one.
- **Heavy** (shingles, tile, plaster)? Don't size up. Heavy loads hit the weight limit before a bigger dumpster fills, so the smaller size is usually the better pick.
- **Expect surprises.** Remodels often turn up old flooring, insulation or framing you didn't plan for.

> *Tip:* Text us a photo of your pile or tell us about the job, and we'll recommend a size.
